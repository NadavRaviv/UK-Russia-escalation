Is the UK–Russia line climbing the same ladder that led to war?

Every event is placed on one rung of an escalation ladder, from a headline to civilians harmed. The five lanes are lined up on the day war broke out, so you can compare the shape of the months before it. UK–Russia is lined up on today, because there is no outbreak to line it up on.

The ladder

Your five labels are rungs 1, 4, 6, 7 and 8. I added diplomatic acts, explicit threats and proxy or covert strikes, because most of the UK–Russia record sits on exactly those rungs and would otherwise be lumped in with headlines.

What the comparison shows

UK–Russia: confirmed rung 5, suspected rung 7

The highest confirmed rung on the British line is 5: British-made drones striking Russia through Ukraine, and a Russian drone hitting a train in Ukraine just after Johnson left. No British and Russian forces have fired on each other. Since 15 August the line has moved into a dense band of explicit threats at rung 3, capped by Putin on 2 September. The two air-traffic failures on 8 and 21 September are plotted at rung 7 as suspected Russian hits. If that were confirmed, the line would skip rung 6 entirely and go straight from threats to civilian infrastructure, the grey-zone pattern that matches none of the other four lanes.

Israel–Iran crossed rung 6 fourteen months early

Before the June 2025 war, the two states had already exchanged direct fire three times in 2024. Direct strikes had become normal before the war itself. The UK–Russia line looks nothing like this lane: it has threats where Israel–Iran had strikes.

Russia–Ukraine: Russia's own war came from posture, not threats

The same actor, fourteen months out, climbed mostly on rung 4: two border build-ups, exercises in Belarus, ships into the Black Sea, then Western drawdowns. Explicit threats were rare, and the rung-2 "pullbacks" were deception. The last week added cyberattacks, a shelling spike and staged evacuations. UK–Russia today is the reverse: many threats, little force moved toward Britain.

US–Iran is the closest shape, minus the tells

Top-level threats, force posture and talks running side by side, much like UK–Russia since August. What changed in the final eleven days: staff drawn down from embassies, a round of talks cancelled, the president telling civilians to leave Tehran, and a public "two weeks" that turned out to be two days. The UK–Russia line has the threats. It does not yet have the drawdown, the forces moved against each other, or talks to cancel.

Israel–Hamas is the warning about quiet lines

The public record nine days before 7 October showed a de-escalation: Erez reopened and border riots stopped after Qatari mediation. The attack plan and the internal warnings never reached open sources. A flat or falling line is not proof of safety, and an OSINT timeline cannot see a deliberate surprise.

Signs from the Ukraine lane

A sign ticks when a confirmed UK–Russia event carries it. A dashed ring means only suspected events do.

    Reading: the same vocabulary, different grammar. Toward Britain, Russia threatens loudly and moves little, which looks like a state warning off a supplier. Toward Ukraine it threatened rarely and moved a great deal, which looked like a state preparing an operation. Most steps up in the UK lane are British; Russia mostly answers in words.

    How this was coded, and its limits

    Each event takes the highest rung its content reaches. A threat stays at rung 3 whoever makes it; authority (a Kremlin adviser against Putin) is noted in the detail, not in the rung. A de-escalating act, such as a ceasefire or Moscow calling the ambassador's departure routine, is placed at rung 2, so the line drops.

    Suspected events are placed at the rung they would reach if the suspicion were true, and drawn with dashed rings and dashed line segments. The two NATS failures are coded this way at your direction: no authority has attributed either to Russia, and for 8 September NATS and the government have said the evidence does not point to a cyberattack. The stats report confirmed and suspected separately.

    The UK–Russia rows are the 33 events in your repository's merged timeline, plus the 21 September failure; the 8 September row was recoded from green to suspected. Rows your colour rule marks green (no direct link to Britain) are hidden by default and shown as hollow points at their natural rung when switched on. They never join the line.

    The four historical lanes were compiled from widely reported events and carry no source links yet. They are a curated sample, not a complete record, so they do not meet your multi-outlet standard. Verify each row before publishing. The carrier and bomber dates in the US–Iran lane are checked against AP and CNN reporting.

    Density is an artefact here too. Every lane crowds near its anchor because attention rose, the same observation effect your README flags. Compare the rungs reached, not the number of points.

    The shapes suggest; they do not predict. Two nuclear-armed states, one inside NATO, do not escalate on the same terms as a state and a non-state group.
